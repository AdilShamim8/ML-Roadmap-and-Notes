# Final Week Preparation Plan — the Last 7 Days, Day by Day

The final week isn't for learning new things — it's for **consolidation, rehearsal, and state management**. Cramming new material now raises anxiety and lowers performance; the plan below is built to peak you exactly at interview time. Each day: a focus, a concrete schedule, and a hard stop. If you're reading this with multiple interviews scheduled, apply the plan to the *next* interview day and treat earlier ones as live rehearsals.

**The two rules of the week:**
1. **Hard stop each day** — 3–4 focused hours, then genuinely off. Exhausted candidates underperform prepared-but-rested ones, reliably.
2. **Everything this week is rehearsal of material you already have** — the notebooks, the stories, the mocks. No new topics. New topics are for after the loop.

---

## Day 7 (One Week Out) — Full Diagnostic Mock

- **Morning (3h):** Run the full mock matching your strongest upcoming loop — [ML Engineer](Mock%20Interview%20-%20ML%20Engineer.md), [Data Scientist](Mock%20Interview%20-%20Data%20Scientist.md), or [System Design](Mock%20Interview%20-%20ML%20System%20Design.md) — timed, graded, honest.
- **Afternoon (1h):** Score it ruthlessly. List every miss by segment. That list is your *entire* study plan for days 6–4; ignore everything you passed.
- **Evening:** off. Non-negotiable.

## Day 6 — Fix the Two Weakest Segments

- Two 90-minute blocks, one per weakest segment from the mock, drilling *the specific misses* (e.g., every window-function question, the STAR stories that rambled, the latency arithmetic you fumbled).
- Rerun just those segments' questions cold at the end of each block.
- **Do not** touch anything you already pass — polishing strengths this week is procrastination in disguise.

## Day 5 — Story Day (Behavioral + Project Deep-Dive)

- **Morning (2h):** Speak all 15 [STAR stories](Behavioral%20and%20STAR%20Answers.md) out loud, timed, 2 minutes each. The failure story and the proudest-impact story twice each.
- **Afternoon (1.5h):** Run the [5-why drill](Project%20Deep-Dive%20Prep.md) on your top two projects, aloud, standing up (it changes your energy — you'll present standing-adjacent energy even seated).
- **Evening:** Write your 90-second pitch once from memory. If it doesn't land on the team-specific closing fact, fix it — then stop thinking about it.

## Day 4 — The Rapid-Fire Gauntlet

- **Morning (2h):** Question banks, cold, fast: the [Question Bank Index](../7.%20Question%20Banks%20and%20Cheat%20Sheets/Question%20Bank%20Index.md) + every "quick-fire" table in the notebooks. Anything you can't answer in 15 seconds gets marked and drilled once more in the afternoon.
- **Afternoon (1h):** The marked misses only. Then the [cheat sheets](../7.%20Question%20Banks%20and%20Cheat%20Sheets/Cheat%20Sheets/) for the two areas with the most marks — reading, not re-deriving.
- **Evening:** Logistics hour — confirm interview times, links, names of interviewers (and one relevant fact per interviewer: their team, a paper, a post — this is tomorrow's small talk and question material).

## Day 3 — One Design Run + Logistics

- **Morning (90 min):** One [system design](Mock%20Interview%20-%20ML%20System%20Design.md) run (or RAG variant if that's the loop), timed, graded, no notes.
- **Afternoon (1h):** Prepare *your* questions for them — 3 per interview, specific to team/project ("how do you monitor drift in production?", "what does the retraining cadence look like?", "what's the current model-vs-rules balance in X?"). Generic questions ("what's the culture like?") waste your strongest signal of genuine interest.
- **Evening:** off, fully. Sleep is a performance enhancer with a dose-response curve; this night matters most.

## Day 2 — Light Rehearsal Only

- **Morning (90 min max):** The pitch, the failure story, the two project one-liners, and ten rapid-fire questions. Nothing new, nothing graded.
- **Afternoon:** environment prep — test the video link, charge devices, lay out clothes, prep the room (door sign if household, water, backup headphones, phone charged for a hotspot emergency).
- **Evening:** normal dinner, normal bedtime. No drills after 8pm — the material is in there; tonight's job is letting it consolidate.

## Day 1 (Interview Eve) — Minimum Viable Day

- **20 minutes, morning:** the pitch once, the failure story once, skim your own resume top to bottom (interviewers ask about *anything* on it — your resume should be as familiar as your stories).
- **Then: normal life.** A walk, a movie, an early dinner. The single most common final-week mistake is a Day-1 cram that trades sleep for anxiety.
- **Sleep target:** 8 hours. Set two alarms. Prepare breakfast plan so the morning has zero decisions.

## Interview Day — the Runbook

**Before each interview:**
- 60 minutes prior: light meal, water, review *only* your question list for them (not your answers — your questions).
- 30 minutes prior: the pitch out loud once, failure story once. Stop.
- 10 minutes prior: standing, shoulders back, three slow breaths. Physiology before psychology.

**During:**
- Clarify before answering anything technical — buying 10 seconds *and* avoiding the wrong question.
- One page of notes per interview is fine (your question list; not scripts).
- When you don't know: "I haven't worked with that — here's how I'd reason about it" and then reason out loud. The reasoning *is* the answer.

**Between rounds (if multi-round day):** 5 minutes each — water, bathroom, two breaths, one-line debrief written down (what was asked — for the debrief later), then reset. No post-mortems mid-day; analysis is for after.

**After:** write the full question list from memory *the same evening* (your future self interviewing elsewhere will thank you), send thank-you notes if that's the norm in the market, then genuinely stop.

---

## The Emergency Compressions

**3 days left?** Day 7's mock + Day 5's stories + Day 2's light rehearsal. Cut everything else.
**1 day left?** Pitch, failure story, project one-liners, ten rapid-fire questions, logistics, sleep. That's the 80/20 — and honestly, for a prepared candidate, it's nearly as good as the full week.
**Multiple loops in one week?** (e.g., onsite Monday, another Friday) — run the full plan before the first; between them, only the day-2 light rehearsal + the missed-questions list from the first loop.

---

## State Management (the Part Nobody Writes Down)

- **Anxiety is load, not signal.** Prepared candidates are nervous too; the ones who perform treat nerves as arousal to channel (fast answers need arousal) rather than danger to suppress.
- **Between-interview rumination is the #1 self-inflicted wound.** A bad round is one data point; candidates who spiral convert one bad round into three. The 5-minute reset between rounds exists to prevent exactly this.
- **The interview is bidirectional.** You are also evaluating them — the questions you prepared are real data collection. Candidates who remember this stop performing desperation and start having a professional conversation, which is the strongest frame there is.
- **Post-loop, whatever happens:** the [negotiation guide](Offer%20Negotiation.md) is the next document if there's an offer, and the question lists you wrote are the beginning of the next cycle's prep if there isn't. Both outcomes have a next step; neither is a verdict on you.
